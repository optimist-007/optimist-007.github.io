This is the part of my work that does not fit neatly into a CV. It shows what I have explored, what I know how to use, and what I am trying to grow into next.

01What I have already explored.From self-powered UV sensing with perovskite/polymer nanocomposites to ferroelectric switching and interfacial charge retention.+
Completed + current

My completed work focused on fluorescent CsPbBr3@Cs4PbBr6/PU perovskite/polymer nanocomposites and their use in a self-powered UV-sensing device. I am now working on ferroelectric switching and interfacial charge retention in thin-film systems, where electrical behaviour and interfaces become the central questions.

What stayed with me

I like research where a material property becomes a measurable device behaviour, and where the interesting part is understanding why that behaviour appears.

02How I am learning to understand it.Experimental characterisation, device measurements and computational tools are becoming one working toolkit rather than separate skills.+
Tools I already use

My experimental toolkit includes thin-film and nanomaterial fabrication, XRD with Rietveld refinement, SEM, AFM, UV-Vis-NIR absorption and Tauc analysis, FTIR, Raman spectroscopy, P-E hysteresis, PUND and DC probe-station measurements. I use Python and OriginPro for analysis, and I have experience with DFT through Quantum ESPRESSO.

What I am building

I want computation to become part of how I ask the next experimental question, not just a separate skill that sits beside the lab work.

03Where I am headed.Toward semiconductor systems, interfaces, sustainable sensing and energy problems, with stronger computational and machine-learning capability.+
Future direction

I want to move deeper into semiconductor materials and interfaces while keeping the experimental grounding that taught me how real materials behave. Sustainable technology, low-carbon energy and sensing remain important anchors, especially where a materials problem connects to a practical environmental or technological constraint.

The direction I am trying to build

Experimental enough to know what is real. Computational enough to ask better questions. Curious enough to keep moving toward harder materials problems.
