Chapter 1: The Door Should't Have Moved

When I opened my eyes, it felt heavy, as if someone had draped a blanket of fog across my thoughts. The evening light filtering through the curtains looked soft, unreal. My limbs felt heavy, my mind floaty, like something inside me hadn’t fully woken up.

Fragments of my memory flickered, cars… a cat… a vintage farmhouse… and then, complete darkness.

I pushed myself up, steadying my breath, and stepped into the hallway. The house was too quiet. Too perfect. As though it was holding its breath.

Familiar soft voices drifted from somewhere far inside. But distant enough to make me question if I was still dreaming. I slapped myself, hard - just to be sure. The sting was immediate.

“Not a dream,” I muttered, rubbing my cheek as I walked towards the living room.

The closer I got, the heavier the air - something had happened. Something I couldn’t fully remember.

A noise from got me back to senses.

Mom was sitting at the counter, writing something, too focused to notice me. I tip-toed behind her.

“BOOO!”

She didn’t even flinch. She simply turned and scanned me the way typical Indian mothers do, checking for fever, injury, hidden crimes or all three.

“You don’t have a fever,” she said. “Do you want to eat something?”

“Didn’t you get scared, mumma?” I pouted, stretching my stiff shoulders. “Also, what happened to me? I just slept for way too long. JK should let me take longer lunch breaks.”

“Sure,” she said dryly. “Or you could resign and get married. Then you can sleep the longer like me.”

I blinked. Her expression didn’t change.

“Mumma … I’m ready,” I whispered dramatically. “Tell JK-”

“Uff, silly girl.” She held a list into my palm. “Go get it.”

It was a grocery list. Chocolate, almonds, bread, milk, cheese, vegetables.

“These are all in the pantry…” I pointed to the hidden door behind her. “Milk and cheese are in the fridge.”

She blinked, embarrassed, she forgot that I literally lived here.

“Eat the neer dosa. Chutney is in that bowl.”

“Did you eat, mumma? You look tired.”

“I’m fine. I’ll go home now.”

“Let me come-”

“Hold that tray and follow me.”

I obeyed. But something felt off. She was acting strange. Quiet.

Outside, JK and Arjun lounged by the pool like two princes sunbathing after conquering an empire. Mom joined them, sipping her tea.

“Mom, where’s Dad?” I asked.

“At home. You stay here tonight. We’ll come tomorrow morning.”

“What? Why can’t I come with you?”

“Because you’re tired. Your father booked you a doctor’s appointment next week. Rest.”

“Pavi!”

Dia came running, hugging me, she got me breathless.

“Dia! I missed you!”

“Missed ME? The taxi driver carried you here earlier! You don’t remember?”

I froze. “What?”

“And YOU didn’t call me,” I shot back.

“You were asleep till now! Anyway, Mom made you some laddoos.”

Before I could touch a single one, JK came in like a starving vulture, grabbed two, stuffed them in his mouth, and tossed the rest to Arjun.

I stared at the empty box.

Dia held her head, “Idiots. Anyway I’m leaving, I have a flight tomorrow.”

But her words echoed strangely.

Taxi driver? Carrying me? Why was I unconscious that long?
